Revision and reanalysis, updated 27 October 2026

The maintained paper is `paper/main.tex`, titled **Finite-cycle sensitivity and phase-resolved amplification in effective loop quantum Bianchi IX cosmology**. The original manuscript, numerical sources, and analysis are preserved in `review/original_before_revision/`. Earlier production data directly under `results/` remain historical and are not used for the revised LQC statistics.

The revision addresses the scientific problems identified in `REVIEW_2026-10-26.md`:

1. **Trajectory-wide validity.** Downward crossings of minimum directional lengths 1, 3, and 10 Planck lengths are tracked throughout every integration segment, including look-ahead and multiple bounces. The first crossing below 1 terminates evolution. Constraint, budget, and other numerical exits are separate. Later failures do not remove an earlier valid event.
2. **A narrower, supported claim.** The paper reports three-cycle outcome sensitivity, phase-resolved amplification over the first complete recollapse-to-recollapse cycle, and a separately controlled six-cycle extension. It no longer claims an asymptotic LQC fractal dimension, an infinite-cycle mixing rate, full-state information preservation, or a black-hole-interior result. The qualitative mechanism is explicitly attributed to earlier work; the contribution is the controlled ensemble quantification.
3. **Correct units and invariance.** Initial rescaling Lambda, measured volume contraction N_V, and mean-scale-factor contraction N_a=N_V/3 are distinct. Event sampling removes a choice of integration clock; finite-time projected distances retain their coordinate and norm choices.
4. **Recomputed statistics.** Each pair's complete-cycle gain is formed before taking the median. All four phase gains and their total use the same cohort. Tables include eligible/resolved counts and 95% percentile-bootstrap intervals. Physical and numerical attrition are reported separately.
5. **Numerical and selection controls.** Every phase pair has two perturbation sizes and a tighter-tolerance unperturbed control. Endpoint selection requires signal/noise at least 100, a relative vector discrepancy of at most 10%, and separation at most 0.1. Supplementary sensitivity statistics use stricter and relaxed quality cuts without changing the primary definition.
6. **Threshold and cutoff checks.** The paper tests length cutoffs 1, 3, and 10. The phase-marker comparison uses thresholds 0.3, 0.5, and 0.7 on one common resolved cohort drawn from the first 200 point IDs. The marker is explicitly a convention, particularly for A quantization; it is not identified with a Hubble rate or a proof of classicality.
7. **Outcome statistics.** Seventeen perturbation radii have saved denominators and Wilson intervals. Zero observed disagreements are displayed with upper confidence bounds. Crossover levels are reported as sampled brackets; no artificial probability floor is used. These are finite-event outcomes, not an LQC dimension estimate.
8. **Equations and reproducibility.** The appendix now specifies W, F_i, and the A evolution equations. The K substitution prefactor is corrected. Raw records have source hashes, cumulative masks, stop reasons, and deterministic seeds. The analysis rejects incomplete production matrices or mismatched simulation sources. Tables and headline numbers are generated from the raw data.

The earlier six-cycle results are not retained as evidence. The revised extension uses 64 base points per model, Double64 arithmetic, two perturbation sizes, and tighter-tolerance controls. High/low components preserve tiny separations consistently at every event. Stepwise gains and fixed six-cycle cohorts are reported separately, with survivor counts. Exploratory and performance pilots with N=2 or 3 are excluded; they contribute no manuscript statistics.

The constraint-residual normalization was subsequently corrected to avoid false alarms when two holonomy sines pass through zero. The full production matrix was rerun with this correction and completed on 27 October. The current source hash distinguishes it from the superseded data under `results/revised_v3_constraint_false_alarms_2026-10-26/`. A regression checks that a formerly rejected cycle now completes with residual below 1e-6 while genuine violations are still detected.

An execution optimization memoizes immutable Vern9 coefficient tables. All three tables are checked byte for byte against the original constructors on every cached launch. A historical pre-normalization-fix comparison found exact agreement in every field of 16 cached/uncached trajectory records; its report is included in `review/validation/cache_historical_2026-10-26.log` and is explicitly distinguished from current validation. The cache source has not changed. Comparing all 512 old/new six-cycle records across the normalization fix gives identical event coordinates, outcomes, and other non-error fields, with changes confined to the normalized residual columns. Current N=64 data were rerun with the cache; they are not described as uncached reference data.

The classical reference is retained with its finite-scale and fitting limitations stated. Its reported D=1.834+/-0.004 is described as an approximate reproduction, not statistically identical to the cited result within the nominal fit errors. It is not presented as a quantum result.

Verification and numerical results are recorded in `results/revised/RESULTS.md`, `summary.json`, and `review/validation/`. The current regression has 27 pathwise checks, including the missed K excursion at two tolerances, an A physical exit, invalid initial constraints, and detection of a genuine constraint violation, plus 3 residual-normalization checks. Five statistical regression tests cover upper bounds at zero counts, median-total aggregation, high/low precision storage, irreversible exits, and withholding a sampling confidence interval for a single pair. `review/validation/VALIDATION.md` identifies current evidence, historical evidence, and source hashes.

Remaining scientific limits: these are conditional finite-time measurements in specified effective Hamiltonians. Directional length cuts do not establish sharply peaked quantum states or control every omitted inverse-triad effect. Numerical resolvability also selects the cohort. A stronger asymptotic chaos claim would require additional return-map or tangent-dynamics work and controlled treatment of exits.

The full requested post-fix production matrix completed on 27 October 2026, including all 256 controlled six-cycle trajectory jobs in each quantization. The current audit passed 52,928 trajectory records (including 16 smoke-test records) and 558,654 saved valid events. The five statistical tests and 27+3 validity checks passed in the readiness review. The earlier equation and baseline verification is described in the manuscript; the release identifies its supplied current audit/regression evidence separately.

At Lambda=20, the resolved first complete recollapse cycle has median gains of 3.81 decades in K (95% interval 3.74-3.89; 503 resolved / 703 eligible pairs) and 3.94 in A (3.84-4.00; 462 / 737). The marker-defined bounce window contributes 0.15 and 0.14 decades, respectively. At the trajectory-wide cutoff of 10 Planck lengths, the full-cycle gains are 4.01 [3.90, 4.08] in K (262 resolved pairs) and 3.97 [3.88, 4.15] in A (283 pairs). The contrast persists under the tested numerical controls.

The six-cycle extension supports a narrower conclusion. K has 9/64 base trajectories completing six cycles, 53 physical exits, and 2 budget exits. Its fixed resolved bounce/recollapse cohorts contain 8/7 pairs, with median per-cycle gains 3.95/3.81 decades. The cut at 3 Planck lengths leaves one pair in each cohort, and the cut at 10 leaves none. A has 29/64 completions, 30 physical exits, and 5 budget exits; its fixed resolved cohorts contain 25/14 pairs, with gains 3.81/3.78 decades. At the cut of 10, only 5/2 pairs remain. These observations cannot establish a robust generic six-cycle rate far from the length boundary or an asymptotic chaos result.

Assessment after revision: the corrected manuscript is a defensible conditional finite-time numerical study, suitable for the author's final scientific and submission review. The original stronger universal/asymptotic and information-preservation claims are not supported. Novelty rests on controlled ensemble quantification and validity/selection analysis, since the qualitative recollapse mechanism was already reported. The manuscript is not a proof of full quantum dynamics, and these checks do not guarantee journal acceptance.

The 27 October readiness revision removes the ancillary persistent-band paragraph. Its claimed cycle gains were differences of eventwise medians on changing samples, and its single-perturbation script omitted the main study's noise, linearity, and separation controls. The claimed exclusion of regular motion and proximity to the full locally rotationally symmetric dynamical subspace were also not established. The exploratory script and data remain preserved locally but are excluded from the submission release. The main production data and generated scientific statistics do not change as a result of this removal.
